# Ground Zero Bill of Standards

The Bill states the promises and working standards of the Ground Zero Development System. The [Constitution](CONSTITUTION.md) defines authority, adoption, amendment and how those promises become operative. The [mechanism map](MECHANISMS.md) distinguishes the tools supplied here from the implementations and evidence owned by an adopting project.

## Articles

### Article I: Human intent and responsible agency

The person directing the work establishes its purpose, consequential choices and delegated bounds. Participants exercise judgment within that authority and carry authorized work through to its intended outcome. Preserve the complete objective, the force of the person's statements and later corrections. A proposed name, architecture or expansion acquires standing through the responsible decision, not through repetition by an agent.

### Article II: Explicit promises and accountable enforcement

An invariant states what the system promises, why it matters, what failure it prevents, who owns it and how conformity is observed. Bind it to a real procedure and responsible owner. A declaration, a carried instruction, a mechanical refusal and an observed result establish different facts. Keep the selected enforcement mechanism and its gaps visible.

### Article III: Grounded acceleration

Development should expand what people can accomplish and understand. Provide an account of what changed, why it works, where it applies and what remains uncertain at the level useful for the decision. Established understanding and applicable evidence reduce repeated work. Process earns its cost through useful delivery, correction and retained capability.

### Article IV: Documentation as operative source

The current record states the intended system and the work being undertaken. Keep purpose, architecture, implementation and receiving connected. Historical decisions and observations remain attributable; current views make the effective state usable. A saved instruction has practical value when a participant or mechanism actually consumes it.

### Article V: Verified outcomes

Make claims at the scope their evidence supports. Select sufficient checks from changed inputs, contracts, dependencies and uncertainty. Verify the actual target and receiving path. Reuse results that remain applicable, preserve contrary evidence and distinguish failure, incompletion and an unavailable observation.

### Article VI: Independent challenge and correction

Use a meaningful independent perspective when the consequence or uncertainty warrants it. Review one coherent result, resolve concrete findings and retain their effect on the work. Challenge assumptions and investigate failure paths without turning disagreement or review volume into an authority of its own. The responsible owner integrates the result and continues delivery.

### Article VII: Applicable obligations at the effect boundary

Identify the rights, privacy, safety, contractual and other applicable obligations that affect the actual work. Obtain appropriate qualified guidance where needed. Bind selected obligations to their owning source, responsible participant and relevant effect. Unrelated work retains its authority while a specific dependency is resolved.

### Article VIII: Transparent method and faithful attribution

Retain enough provenance to explain an action, observation, inference and decision. Attribute sources, participants, tools and generated assessments honestly. Record actual outcomes and measurement limits. Preserve useful history while keeping the current artifact focused on its purpose.

### Article IX: Private data and secret custody

Credentials and private operational data remain in the selected authorized storage and are exposed only where an operation requires them. Source and ordinary records use appropriate references and synthetic examples. Publication receives its own content and reachable-history review. A copied record or credential reference is not a grant to use the underlying resource.

## Standards of practice

### Standard 1: Scope and delegation

Before substantive work, identify the accepted outcome, its parent, the responsible owner, the selected source, permitted changes, constraints, dependencies and required return. Scale the record to the engagement. Routine work within that scope uses existing authorization; a material change in purpose, priority, architecture or authority follows the responsible decision path.

A delegation carries these relationships to its recipient and rejoins the result with the coordinating owner. A direct maintenance dependency names the affected contract, bounded repair and return to delivery. Independent improvement keeps its own work and priority. Completing the repair does not replace the original objective.

Where practices have configurable defaults, apply explicit operator direction within its authority, then selected project or team practice, delegated participant settings and product defaults. Version maturity supplies a baseline input rather than authority over an explicit choice. A default does not reinterpret the accepted goal or weaken an applicable promise.

### Standard 2: Registered invariants

Register a consequential promise before relying on it. Name the statement, source decision, affected subjects, failure prevented, owner and verification method. Describe the current enforcement standing: carried guidance, deterministic check, effect-boundary gate, human judgment or a combination. A mechanism that does not exist remains an implementation gap rather than an enforcement claim.

Amend or retire an adopted invariant through a new attributable decision. Preserve its earlier scope, observations and any unfinished consumer transition.

### Standard 3: Purpose, architecture and implementation

Explain each substantial governed component at three connected levels: the purpose it serves, its responsibilities and relationships, and the concrete source or operation that realizes it. A reader should be able to move between those levels. Resolve a mismatch in the owning source or behavior, then update the account.

### Standard 4: Failure and propagation

For consequential boundaries, describe what fails first, how the failure reaches dependent consumers and which authority or integrity boundary is affected. Include adversarial or malformed conditions when relevant. Use that account to select detecting tests and recovery behavior. A list of successful cases alone does not establish the boundary.

### Standard 5: Specification and current state

State the accepted behavior, scope and required integration before implementing a material change. Use the existing owning record rather than requiring a new document for every edit. Record later corrections and the actual order of events honestly. A specification supplied after implementation cannot retroactively authorize an earlier effect.

Specifications state the canonical shape directly. Explanation, rationale and examples teach the subject where useful. Keep non-selected alternatives in decision context or a scoped boundary when they prevent a likely mistake. Maintain one ordinary source document per subject; generate other formats for a real requested use.

### Standard 6: Verification and evidence applicability

Acceptance requires sufficient evidence for the selected scope, applicable promises and actual receiving path. Before verification, identify the claim, changed inputs, affected contracts, dependencies, relevant environment and material uncertainty. Each check or review should resolve a useful question.

Preserve evidence whose relevant source, method, inputs, dependencies and environment remain applicable. Retain the observed status and a retrievable result. A new commit, timestamp or publication event alone does not invalidate it. Refresh time-dependent observations when their freshness matters.

A changed input or contrary finding reopens the coverage it can affect. Keep independent passing results when another check fails. Report failed, incomplete, skipped, unavailable and invalidated evidence distinctly. Broaden verification when dependencies cannot be bounded, shared behavior changes, a defect reveals wider reach or required project policy calls for it.

Observe behavior through the relevant boundary. Source inspection, a build, installation, an application result and a human assessment support different claims. Once sufficient evidence resolves the selected delivery question, record it and continue delivery.

### Standard 7: Applicable regulatory and contractual constraints

Identify obligations from the work's actual jurisdiction, rights and contracts. Record their source and interpretation separately from the mechanism proposed to satisfy them. The responsible owner receives the implementation and its evidence. A general checklist neither grants permission nor proves compliance.

### Standard 8: Method and data provenance

Record what was selected, performed, observed and inferred, with the source and conditions that matter. Keep original human feedback distinguishable from automated assessment. Preserve failed and contrary results alongside successful ones. Report available resource, cost and performance measurements in their actual units and identify missing coverage.

### Standard 9: Reproduction and uncertainty

Preserve enough inputs, revisions, methods and environment information to reproduce or meaningfully compare a result. Distinguish deterministic computation from variable model or environmental behavior. A reproducible procedure does not imply identical model output; a correlation or confidence estimate does not independently establish truth.

Use bounded investigations when uncertainty affects a useful next decision. State the question, expected information, resources and stopping condition. Rejoin the result to the work that gives it purpose.

### Standard 10: Continuous evaluation and correction

Development and operation produce data for evaluating the system's behavior and its methods. Connect a sourced observation to an interpretation, a responsible decision, an authorized correction and an observed consequence. Preserve what was observed, inferred, unknown and contradicted. A logged finding or generated suggestion does not establish that the next action changed.

Assess changes against current objectives and invariants using Standard 6. Compare the actual result with relevant original intent and later corrections. Reuse settled knowledge; deepen investigation where new interactions or uncertainty require it. A correction to policy, source, routing or procedure reaches completion through its intended consumer and subsequent use.

### Standard 11: Memory and continuity

Keep conceptual definition, planning/history and current operational state distinct and connected. Name the actual durable stores, records, orientation documents and private lanes that carry them. An orientation file can point to memory; its presence does not prove that durable state was saved or loaded.

At continuation, reconstruct the mandate, source, decisions, ownership, evidence, unfinished work and next eligible action from current records. Verify changed conditions rather than assuming an earlier handoff is current. A new session, host or model retains existing project obligations and participants. Respect deliberately absent prior context where the project selects it.

### Standard 12: Repository and source authority

Verify the actual repository root, branch or worktree, selected source and remote before a material source or publication operation. Preserve meaningful existing history, working changes and independent repository boundaries. Repository, project, source owner and session are different identities.

A source transfer identifies the selected base and working deltas, producer, receiver, permitted differences and update path. Reconcile imports, builds, consumers and navigation together. A new clone or clean baseline does not silently replace the current source. Publication scope and private-source preservation are separate decisions.

### Standard 13: Odometer and once-only credit

Assess version movement against the full accepted feature or module. Patch corrects an existing behavior; kohai records coherent progress toward unfinished scope; minor records completed coherent scope with required integration and sufficient evidence; major is selected for a completed related grouping, release or identity boundary. Hotfix uses the patch coordinate. Record the scope, parent, evidence and credit owner before computing a value.

Credit a delivery once. A new API, artifact, child publication or PR does not by itself establish feature completion. Reorganization and repeated aggregation receive no additional credit. Preserve original labels, source and published values when reconciling historical accounting.

The disclosed Ground Zero odometer uses `major.minor.kohai.patch` with optional maturity suffix. Its hard caps are minor 12, kohai 16 and patch 24; carries reset lower coordinates and propagate upward. Coordinate 10 is informational. Mechanical carry does not declare a feature, release or maturity level complete. The [calculator](../scripts/odometer.mjs) computes the arithmetic and preserves a suffix; the responsible owner selects the evidenced tier and any maturity change. An adopting project selects its baseline and records compatibility policy separately.

### Standard 14: Coherent responsibilities and work

Before adding a consequential component, role, skill or other responsibility, inspect the existing system, its owners, revisions, contracts and demonstrated behavior. Diagnose a missing capability separately from failure to apply an existing one. Reuse or repair an adequate responsibility. A new one records the remaining gap, why existing responsibilities cannot meet it, its boundary and owner before the creation action.

A consumer enforcing this creation contract checks its required facts before dispatch or writing the new surface. Missing required facts or an unavailable required gate must not silently admit creation. A post-task critique cannot substitute for that selected pre-action boundary. The [mechanism map](MECHANISMS.md) states which implementation owns the actual enforcement.

Keep the accepted module unified under its parent outcome. Use coherent children where useful, recording reason, scope, owner, contracts, dependencies and required closure evidence before implementation. Child completion retains the unfinished parent. Close the parent when its full scope and integration obligations are satisfied.

Granular, Emmetropic and Beacon express contextual development postures: smaller useful increments, substantive connected outcomes, and broad scope with its relationships. The working and delivery boundaries determine their use. They impose no fixed quotas, task categories, permissions or version tiers. Record a material change in organization with its reason, retained goals, evidence, dependencies and unfinished membership.

### Standard 15: Attribution and participant identity

Public development records center the responsible human, source contribution and developed system. Commits, pull requests, releases and project-facing attribution do not add AI-provider co-author trailers, generated-by footers, imposed model or vendor labels, or promotional boilerplate.

Name a provider or tool where it serves an actual technical purpose, such as an API adapter, compatibility requirement or interpretation of evidence. Preserve required third-party notices. Retain detailed tool and model provenance in the appropriate operational or evidence record rather than using it as routine authorship decoration.

Identity, role, model, host and execution arrangement retain their separate meanings. Attribute the actual contribution honestly, without impersonating a person or presenting generated commentary as their original judgment. Keep personal and account details within their proper disclosure boundary.

### Standard 16: Tool and runtime boundaries

Expose capabilities through explicit contracts appropriate to their consumers. Bind the selected actor, work, source, resource and operation at the actual effect boundary. Treat permission, currentness, input validity, custody and outcome as distinct checks. A transport success, serialized identifier or policy-file hash does not establish authorization or semantic acceptance.

When an effect is uncertain, preserve its original attempt and reconcile it before a potentially duplicating retry. A host or engine implements the actual controls; Ground Zero supplies the selected obligations and conformance requirements. Its documentation does not create a running gate.

### Standard 17: Independent release and adoption

Each project owns its source, version and delivery records, contributions, checks and release decisions. An adopted standards relationship does not make two repositories one project. Receive selected policy and mechanism revisions explicitly, with their applicable scope and unresolved obligations.

Use the project's established branch, review and publication path. A required failing check is a concrete delivery dependency: correct the responsible behavior, retain applicable evidence and return to delivery. Keep source, local state, generated outputs and publication history within their selected boundaries. A release changes the selected distribution; it does not make private development history or future concepts public by implication.
